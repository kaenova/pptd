# Finance Design Baseline

Shared requirements for finance presets. Read this file with exactly one selected preset's `design.md`. That preset's PART B overrides this baseline where they conflict; do not combine PART B sections from different presets.

**Full creative commitment**: treat each page as a complete design work. Structure, composition, charts, imagery, and visual narrative must follow content rather than default layouts.

**No AI slop**: avoid templated decoration, unjustified card arrays, evenly split grids, gratuitous gradients or glassmorphism, icon walls, hollow oversized type, repeated skeletons, and visual gimmicks unrelated to content. Every page needs a clear content-based design rationale.

**Landscape delivery**: final deck uses 16:9 landscape. Recompose portrait or other-ratio source material; do not preserve its canvas ratio.

**Layout coordination**: the selected preset governs final visual rules. The per-page layout/visual-task instruction governs structure, primary anchor, and reading order. Follow both.

## Global Prohibitions

1. No default cards: do not use rounded rectangles or rectangular cards to build hierarchy or alignment unless requested. Functional information containers are allowed. Prefer rules, whitespace, and type hierarchy.
2. No unjustified equal splits: avoid default three-way, four-way, or 2×2 layouts unless content warrants them.
3. No generic AI palettes: avoid unjustified blue-purple gradients, cyan-purple neon, rainbow flares, glass cards, and glowing borders.
4. No elements that clash with selected preset's visual language.
5. Keep chart backgrounds transparent or matching page background, except reversed treatments on dark grounds.
6. Avoid large empty areas and artificial padding; keep reasonable evidence density without stuffing content to fill space.

## Finance Category Baseline

Institutional investment research, due diligence, and strategy outlooks use traceable numbers, explicit assumptions, and actionable conclusions. Tables carry financial-model arguments; charts support them. Keep information density high but ordered, with each page answering one investment-committee question.

### Content and layout

1. Thesis-led sequence: transaction decks move through summary, investment highlights, target/market, business plan and financial diagnosis, valuation and returns, risks and mitigants, and decision request. Research decks move from core stance through evidence and scenarios to allocation recommendation. End with an explicit decision or stance.
2. Financial tables are central: valuation ranges, comparables, three-statement models, scenarios, and sensitivity grids use disciplined rows and columns, with assumptions, drivers, and checks clearly distinguished. Charts (valuation ranges, IRR bridges, waterfalls, scenario fans) serve data, never decorate.
3. Use multi-column layouts with clear hierarchy. Density comes from evidence modules, not walls of text.

### Visual discipline

1. Keep aesthetics restrained, authoritative, and compliance-clean. No lifestyle photography, decorative icons, or marketing superlatives.
2. Use serif titles only when selected preset calls for gravitas; use sans serif for table body, chart labels, and numerals. Align numbers by column.
3. Use conservative institutional structural colors and restrained accents. Keep positive/negative colors semantically consistent and meaningful unless selected preset overrides them.
4. Use fine table rules, precise vector chart lines, and thin bridge-chart connectors. Avoid 3D, shadows, and routine gradient fills.

### Evidence and structure

1. Body titles state complete investment assertions, with a subtitle defining scope and how to read page.
2. Every number carries unit, as-of date, and basis. Label estimates; no bare numbers.
3. Present returns as bear/base/bull ranges with probabilities or driving assumptions, not single-point IRR. Pair valuation conclusions with sensitivity analysis.
4. Make assumptions explicit where conclusions depend on them.
5. Pair each risk with probability/impact, mitigation, and owner/status.
6. Anchor valuation with comparable companies, precedent transactions, or DCF cross-check.
7. Parallel evidence may use 2–3 columns; numbered arguments carry one-line claim and short proof.
8. Label chart marks directly, annotate bridge blocks, distinguish forecast segments, and name key drivers in charts. Do not rely on legends alone.
9. End with explicit decision or allocation stance, next actions, and timeline; no thank-you close.
10. Enlarge key metrics relative to body copy and keep units/basis adjacent. Include a high-density financial table when relevant; align numbers, use consistent separators and negative-number conventions, distinguish forecast from history, and cite source/basis/time range in page footer.
11. Keep one visual temperament per deck, as defined by selected preset.
12. Title read-through test: page titles alone should tell complete story.
13. Avoid large blank areas, but allow whitespace for breathing, grouping, and rhythm.

### Density

Follow selected preset's Density Baseline for page-specific evidence modules, charts, and annotations. No universal numeric floor.
